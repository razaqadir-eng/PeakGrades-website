⏱️ Exam technique & preparation

GCSE Physics exam guide.

MathsEnglishBiologyChemistryPhysics

On AQA, separate Physics is two papers of 1 hour 45 minutes (100 marks each); Combined Science has two Physics papers of 1 hour 15 minutes (70 marks each). Physics is the most calculation-heavy science, so technique matters as much as knowledge.

Paper formats use AQA as the reference board — always check your own specification.

Timing strategy

  • Budget about 1 minute per mark, with 5–10 minutes at the end to recheck calculations and units.
  • Calculations worth 2–3 marks should take 2–4 minutes. If one is taking longer, write the equation and move on.
  • Allow 6–8 minutes for 6-mark questions, which often ask you to describe a practical method or explain a process.
  • Read the whole question before calculating. Units and the quantity asked for are often in the final line.
  • Check that answers are physically sensible: a car travelling at 3,000 m/s means something has gone wrong.

Showing working

  • Use the same four lines for every calculation:
    1. Write the equation in symbols or words
    2. Substitute the numbers, converted to SI units
    3. Rearrange if needed
    4. Give the answer with units
  • Convert units first: kJ to J, g to kg, minutes to seconds, cm to m.
  • Rearrange before substituting if you find it easier; either way, show the rearranged form.
  • Error carried forward applies, so always use your earlier answer in later parts, even if you're unsure of it.
  • Explanations need a chain: cause → mechanism → effect, using correct terms (resultant force, current, potential difference).
  • Graphs: use a sharp pencil, plot accurately, draw a line or curve of best fit, and show gradient working on the graph.

Calculator use

  • Standard form and prefixes: know kilo (10³), mega (10⁶), giga (10⁹), milli (10⁻³), micro (10⁻⁶) and nano (10⁻⁹).
  • Powers and roots: square roots appear in kinetic energy and v² − u² = 2as calculations.
  • Use brackets when substituting into equations with several terms.
  • Keep full values until the final step, then round to the precision asked (usually 2–3 significant figures).
  • Gradients and areas under graphs: acceleration from a velocity–time graph, distance from the area under it.
  • Equations sheet: check which equations are provided for your exam year and learn the rest by heart.
